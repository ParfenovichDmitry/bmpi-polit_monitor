How to read this page

This monitor applies the diagnostic protocol from the article “Hidden media reversal in election campaigns: regime changes in the media field across nine elections (2016–2026)” to a running campaign. It measures how the news media field treats two contenders day by day. It is a diagnostic instrument, like a poll of the media rather than of voters: it describes the state of coverage, not the election result.

1. What is measured

Every 15 minutes GDELT publishes machine-coded metadata (GKG 2.1) for news articles worldwide. The bot keeps articles that mention an actor through the configured keyword list and records the article’s average tone (first element of V2Tone; negative = unfavourable wording). Two streams are collected: the English stream (restricted to US domestic outlets for US campaigns) and the Translingual stream (non-English press, machine-translated by GDELT).

2. From tone to pressure: pBMPI

z(t) = (tone(t) − mean_30d) / sd_30d rolling z-score, W = 30 days, ≥ 15 obs, clipped to ±4 L(t) = 1 / (1 + e^z(t)) pBMPI, bounded in (0, 1) ΔpBMPI = −(L_A − L_B) field balance between the two actors

L is the BMPI “lens” carried over from the financial-market version of the index (BMPIt = σ((1−w)·zmentions + w·(−ztone)) with w = 1, i.e. tone only). Only the window changes: 30 days instead of 365, because campaigns are short. L > 0.5 means today’s coverage is harsher than the actor’s own last 30 days; L < 0.5 means it is milder. Each actor is compared with itself, so a party that is always covered negatively is not penalised for that baseline.

ΔpBMPI > 0: the field is tilted in favour of actor A (A is under relatively less pressure than B). ΔpBMPI < 0: tilted in favour of B. Values near 0: balanced.

5. Limits of interpretation

  • Diagnostic, not a forecast. The indicators describe media coverage. In the article’s nine elections a significant hidden reversal was found once (USA 2016); H4 matched 7 of 8 outcomes but is not statistically established (Fisher p = 0.107).
  • Daily re-testing. The change-point test is recomputed every day on a growing window. Watching a p-value every day inflates false alarms; treat intermediate p-values as descriptive. The confirmatory reading is the one computed on the complete window after the last pre-election day.
  • Favourite is set manually from public polls (config), following the article’s rule (mean of polls in the 30 days before the window). It is not scraped automatically.
  • Coverage. GDELT over-represents English-language and online media; the Translingual stream reflects foreign coverage, which can be dominated by one actor (e.g. a sitting president’s party). Low daily counts make single days noisy — read 5–10 day patterns, not single bars.
  • Multi-seat contests (e.g. a legislature) are reduced to two blocs; district-level dynamics are invisible here.
  • Media coverage in systems with a non-free or partly free press may not satisfy the method’s assumptions; extreme asymmetry of coverage is a signal of limited pluralism, not by itself an explanation of it.

6. Data and reproducibility

Source: GDELT Project, GKG 2.1 (English and Translingual 15-minute files). Every run stores the matched mentions and a per-interval download log on the server; the page shows the share of missing 15-minute intervals. Code, configuration and the full method are in the repository.
